**This calculator is an educational tool. It is not financial, tax, investment, or legal advice, and
it is not a substitute for a qualified professional.** It claims no endorsement by any financial
educator or firm.

## Simulation results are probabilities, not guarantees

Every result is produced by a *model* under assumptions you can edit. A "90% probability of success"
means that in 90% of the simulated futures — given your assumptions — the goal was met. It is not a
promise, and it does not account for risks the model omits. Small changes in return, inflation, or
longevity assumptions can move the result more than the difference between two success percentages.

## Country tax profiles are simplified examples

The built-in country profiles (United States, Italy, United Kingdom, Canada, France, Germany, Spain,
Portugal, Switzerland, Australia, and a custom placeholder) are **simplified illustrations for
scenario planning**, not authoritative tax calculations. Real cross-border taxation depends on:

- Tax treaties and their specific articles for each income type.
- Residency and domicile rules, and the timing of transitions.
- Whether an account type is *recognized* abroad — for example, a Roth IRA or HSA is often **not**
  treated as tax-free outside the United States; an ISA is tax-free only in the UK; a TFSA only in
  Canada; superannuation rules are specific to Australia.
- Wealth taxes, social charges, exit/entry taxes, deemed-disposition rules, and special regimes
  (e.g. Italy's regional flat tax, Portugal's NHR/IFICI, Spain's Beckham regime, Swiss lump-sum
  taxation) that change frequently and have eligibility conditions.
- Estate and inheritance taxes, which vary enormously by jurisdiction.

The profile notes flag several of these, but they are **not** exhaustive and may become outdated.
Treat all international modeling here as **scenario planning**, and verify current rules with a
cross-border tax professional before acting.

## Taxes are modeled at a simplified level

Even the domestic marginal-bracket mode is simplified: it uses illustrative brackets, treats Social
Security as up to 85% taxable, stacks preferential capital-gains rates in a simplified way, and does
not model every credit, phase-out, surtax, or state-specific rule. The flat mode is intentionally a
single effective rate. Use these to explore *directional* effects, not to compute an actual tax
return.

## Fees and value of advice

The tool quantifies the compounding drag of fees but does **not** assume an advisor's value is zero.
The "value of advice" you enter is a **subjective assumption** you provide, clearly labeled as such.
Separate quantifiable investment/tax effects from behavioral, planning, and convenience value when
interpreting the comparison.
